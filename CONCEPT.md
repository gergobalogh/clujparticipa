# ClujParticipa - Concept Note

## The Problem

Cluj-Napoca has no widely trusted, structured way for residents to propose, refine, and follow neighborhood improvements through to implementation. Citizen input is scattered across social media, occasional consultations, complaint channels, and informal networks. When participation is disconnected from visible decisions and delivery, residents learn that engagement does not matter.

## The Precedent

Vantaa, Finland runs participatory budgeting through Osallistuva Vantaa. Residents submit ideas, the city turns feasible ideas into proposals, residents vote, and winning projects are implemented and tracked. Vantaa announced EUR 1.2 million for participatory budgeting in 2023-2024, with implementation of selected ideas in 2024-2025. In the 2024 vote, residents aged 13 and over could vote on proposals within regional budget limits.

## The Adaptation

Romania is not Finland. Institutional trust is lower, politicization risk is higher, and civic infrastructure is thinner. A direct copy would fail. ClujParticipa should adapt the model with three constraints:

1. **District-first, not citywide.** Start with a single neighborhood pilot such as Manastur, then expand only after the workflow proves credible.
2. **Radical transparency.** Publish proposal status, budgets, feasibility notes, procurement links where available, implementation timelines, and public audit history.
3. **Anti-capture design.** Use proposal cost caps, clear eligibility rules, transparent moderation, rate limits, public status history, and a path toward stronger identity verification before any binding vote.

## The Pilot

The first prototype should be a lightweight, mobile-first platform for one Cluj district. Residents can:

- Submit ideas with location, category, and description
- Browse ideas by category, status, and district
- Vote or signal support in a clearly non-binding prototype mode
- Comment and discuss under moderation rules
- Track implementation status through visible updates

The platform should be trilingual: Romanian, Hungarian, and English. It should be open source and based on free/open infrastructure where practical: Leaflet, OpenStreetMap, PostgreSQL, and standard web hosting.

## Why Cluj

Cluj-Napoca has unusually good conditions for a Romanian civic technology pilot: a large student population, strong technical community, active civil society, universities, and a public identity around urban innovation. That does not guarantee institutional adoption, but it creates enough local capacity to build a credible prototype and coalition.

## Funding Path

EU funding is relevant, but the applicant model matters. The European Urban Initiative can fund city-led urban innovation projects with up to EUR 5 million ERDF and up to 80% co-financing. CERV can support democratic participation and civil society work. URBACT supports city networks, transfer, and action planning.

For an independent software developer, the realistic funding path is not to apply alone for a large city grant. The realistic path is to build a strong prototype, document the model, and use it to assemble a city/NGO/university partnership that can pursue funding.

## The Ask

Build the prototype and use it as a conversation artifact. The first objective is not citywide deployment. The first objective is to make the idea concrete enough that local partners can evaluate, improve, and potentially adopt it.
